01 / Building the scale

Turning an idea about everyday happiness into measurable items

Working in a four-member undergraduate research team, I co-developed an initial 26-item pool intended to capture happiness-related habits. Data from 163 young adults were analyzed in SPSS 20.0 using item analysis, internal-consistency analysis, and exploratory factor analysis.

The initial 26-item measure demonstrated Cronbach’s α = .808. Criterion-related validity was examined through its relationship with the Satisfaction With Life Scale, showing a significant positive association (r = .399, p < .001).

02 / What emerged

A 15-item, three-factor measure

The scale-development process resulted in a 15-item measure organized around three factors. Rather than treating happiness as a single feeling, the final structure captured patterns related to acting intentionally, interpreting adversity, and relating to other people.

03 / From research to practice

From psychometric study to an applied assessment portfolio

Following the scale-development work, Sky-Dream Coaching Psychology Center designed a physical cover and response sheet around the Happiness Habit Scale. A separate projective happiness activity was also incorporated as a complementary qualitative component, bringing the materials together as an applied assessment portfolio.

This later portfolio illustrates how a research product can move into practice while still requiring a clear distinction between what was psychometrically examined and what was added for exploratory use.

Methodological distinction Only the self-report Happiness Habit Scale underwent the reliability, factor-analytic, and criterion-related validity analyses reported in the 2019 study. The projective component is presented here as a complementary exploratory activity and is not described as a separately standardized or validated psychological test.
