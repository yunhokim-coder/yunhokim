Poom Family: Coaching That Begins at Home

A home visit can reveal the everyday context behind a young person’s goals. The Poom Family homecare coaching project brought child and caregiver support into that context, in partnership with a community welfare center.

01 / The setting

For children growing up amid economic strain or changing family circumstances, schoolwork is only part of the story. Study routines, relationships, and confidence take shape at home. Working with SkyDream Coaching Psychology Center and a community welfare center in Gimpo, I joined a project serving upper-elementary and middle-school youth from multicultural, single-parent, and economically vulnerable families.

The welfare center helped select families and connect them with practical community resources, including support for everyday needs. Our psychology team visited homes to offer child coaching, psychological assessment and interpretation, caregiver coaching, and family activities. The project also included parent education sessions.

A goal becomes more reachable when the people and routines around it are part of the conversation.
02 / The work

Listening to the whole family

In my role as a counselor and program evaluator, I looked at how a child’s motivation, peer relationships, and daily routines related to the caregiver’s perspective and the home environment. Coaching sessions translated broad hopes into smaller goals that a young person and their caregiver could discuss and revisit together.

The team used assessment to inform conversations with families and examined pre–post program data internally. These observations helped us reflect on the service; they do not establish a causal effect or stand in for each family’s experience.

03 / Beyond the visit

Community support is part of the picture

Home coaching could address conversations and habits within a family, while the welfare center helped connect families to resources beyond the visit. The experience sharpened my interest in how family, community, and school support can work together. It also made a practical gap visible: the project had no direct school partnership, so changes at home were not consistently connected to classroom support.
